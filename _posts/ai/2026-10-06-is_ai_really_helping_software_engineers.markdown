Lately I've been thinking a lot about how AI is helping me, not just to work, but also to manage and to learn new things. So here are my thoughts on three areas: learning, working, and managing.

### Learning

Learning is one of the most important things we have as humans, because everything we do comes from what we have learned. So if AI can help me learn new things, it helps me figure out how to do one thing and how to handle everything that comes after it.

And it has been working well. Most of the time, when I have a YouTube video to watch, I copy the link, send it to [NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/), and ask it to summarize the video and explain what it is about, who is talking, and why.

I do the same with books. I'm reading *Nothing More to Tell*, for example. I took the PDF, sent it to NotebookLM, and asked it to summarize the chapters and help me understand the book. It can even create a video or an audio overview from it. I'm doing this to learn English and to learn other things too, because it's a good way to learn.

Of course, I didn't stop learning on my own, by reading or watching movies. But to summarize, to get some ideas into my head, to understand something hard, or to explain it to others, AI is helping me a lot. Some things are really hard to understand at first, and it helps me get them.

### Working

I'm a software engineer with a decade of experience, and one of the most important parts of my current job is understanding problems and finding solutions. I also spend a lot of time in meetings. So when I finally get to the code, I need to type it fast, because I need to deliver the solution fast.

That's where AI has been *mão na roda*, as we say in Fortaleza, Ceará — a real helping hand. It writes a lot of code much faster than I could.

But the most important part is still finding the solution. I need to think, and sometimes AI helps me think. It helps me understand the problem faster, solve it faster, deliver it faster, and, if something else breaks, understand that new problem and fix it quickly too.

Even when I use AI to code, I'm still responsible for the result. I still have to read the code, sometimes change it myself, check the UI, check the unit tests, and do everything else that matters to a software engineer.

When I want to learn something new about software development, of course I'm going to code it myself. But for a side project just to play around, like building a game, it's really easy to tell the AI "build this", check the result, and if it's not working properly, tell it what's wrong so it can fix it.

### Managing

Another part of my job is managing the team's tasks. I'm not a manager, I'm a software architect, but sometimes I need to handle the team and organize their work.

What I do is take the tasks I've already discussed with the clients, write the documentation, and explain them to the trainees and junior developers I mentor. They try to understand, come to me with their questions, and then they write the code.

The question I always ask them is: *what did you learn in this task?* When you are building something, it's really important to know **why** and **how** you are doing it, and how it works. That's what sets you apart, because a lot of people just want to deliver something. The most important thing is to understand the problem, understand how to solve it, and know why you are solving it that way, because there are many different ways to solve the same problem.

That's what software engineering is: choosing why and how. Every choice is a trade-off, and it will impact something else. So you need to know what the trade-off is and why you (or the team) chose it.

### So, is it really helping?

I think AI is helping us software engineers a lot, but we need to be careful not to hand our responsibility over to it. AI won't talk to our clients for us, and it won't understand and solve the problem for us. Understanding and solving the problem is a human responsibility.

Of course, you can use AI to get better at that, like I do. In a meeting, I talk with the clients and understand the problem. Then, to find a solution, I sometimes ask the AI: "What are the ways I could solve this?" I think that's a really interesting way to improve our lives, and I'm going to keep learning new things with AI's help.
